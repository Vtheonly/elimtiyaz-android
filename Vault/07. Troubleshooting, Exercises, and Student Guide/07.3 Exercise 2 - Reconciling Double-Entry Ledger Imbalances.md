## 1. Problem Scenario

During the monthly financial audit of October 2026, the chief accountant observes that the trial balance for the parent account of **M. Slimani (PAR-2026-0045)** is out of balance.

### Existing Corrupted Journal Entries:
1. **Entry 1 (2026-09-01)**: Tuition charge for Student Karim (4AP) was recorded as:
   - Debit `1200 - Accounts Receivable`: $295,000\text{ DZD}$
   - Credit `4010 - Tuition Revenue`: $295,000\text{ DZD}$
2. **Entry 2 (2026-09-15)**: Cash payment of Tranche 1 ($118,000\text{ DZD}$) was erroneously entered twice by two staff cashiers:
   - *Receipt A*: Debit `1010 - Cash on Hand` $118,000\text{ DZD}$, Credit `1200 - Accounts Receivable` $118,000\text{ DZD}$.
   - *Receipt B (Duplicate)*: Debit `1010 - Cash on Hand` $118,000\text{ DZD}$, Credit `1200 - Accounts Receivable` $118,000\text{ DZD}$.

### Consequence:
The parent's balance shows:
$$\text{Calculated Balance} = 295,000 - 118,000 - 118,000 = 59,000\text{ DZD}$$
Whereas the true remaining balance owed is:
$$\text{True Balance} = 295,000 - 118,000 = 177,000\text{ DZD}$$
Furthermore, the cash register at the end of the day was short by $118,000\text{ DZD}$ relative to the ledger!

---

## 2. Requirement
Formulate the precise, non-destructive accounting correction entries to reconcile the general ledger without deleting any database records.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Solution

### 3.1 Step 1: Identify the Duplicate Transaction
- Target Duplicate: Receipt B (ID: `pay-2026-0899`, amount: $11,800,000\text{ centimes}$).

### 3.2 Step 2: Formulate the Balancing Reversal Entry
Because financial records are immutable, we insert an exact inverse entry referencing `reversesId = pay-2026-0899`:

- **Debit**: `1200 - Accounts Receivable (Créances Parents)`: $+11,800,000\text{ centimes}$ ($118,000\text{ DZD}$)
- **Credit**: `1010 - Cash on Hand (Caisse)`: $+11,800,000\text{ centimes}$ ($118,000\text{ DZD}$)
- **Reverses Reference ID**: `pay-2026-0899`
- **Audit Note**: "Reversal of duplicate cashier entry REC-2026-00412. Authorized by Chief Accountant."

---

## 4. Post-Reconciliation Verification

### Parent Ledger Summary for M. Slimani:
$$\sum \text{Debits} = 295,000\text{ DZD} \text{ (Initial Charge)} + 118,000\text{ DZD} \text{ (Reversal)} = 413,000\text{ DZD}$$
$$\sum \text{Credits} = 118,000\text{ DZD} \text{ (Receipt A)} + 118,000\text{ DZD} \text{ (Receipt B)} = 236,000\text{ DZD}$$
$$\text{Net Receivable} = 413,000 - 236,000 = \mathbf{177,000\text{ DZD}}$$

The remaining parent balance matches the exact expected unpaid Tranches 2 and 3 ($88,500 + 88,500 = 177,000\text{ DZD}$).

### Cash Register Balance:
$$\sum \text{Debits (Physical Cash Received)} - \sum \text{Credits (Reversal)} = (118,000 + 118,000) - 118,000 = \mathbf{118,000\text{ DZD}}$$
The ledger cash account matches the actual physical bank deposit bag!

---

## 5. Tips and Common Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> **Student Trap: Using SQL `DELETE FROM payments WHERE id = ...`.**
> Executing a raw SQL delete removes the audit trail. If a bank auditor asks why physical cash did not match the initial ledger receipts on September 15, the accounting staff will have zero proof. Always record an explicit reversal entry!
